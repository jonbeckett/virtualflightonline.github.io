# Getting started with MSFS

Microsoft Flight Simulator 2020 and 2024 are the most popular simulators in the VFO community. This guide walks you through getting the sim set up and flying your first flight.

## Getting the simulator

Microsoft Flight Simulator is available through:

- **Microsoft Store** (direct from Microsoft)
- **Steam**
- **Xbox Game Pass / PC Game Pass** (subscription)

The base game includes a large selection of aircraft and airports. Payware and freeware add-ons can expand the aircraft and scenery library significantly — see [Resources](../resources/) for recommendations.

## System requirements

MSFS is demanding on hardware. For a good VFO group flight experience, Microsoft recommends at least:

| Component | Recommended |
| --- | --- |
| CPU | Intel Core i7-9800X / AMD Ryzen 7 Pro 2700X |
| RAM | 32 GB |
| GPU | NVIDIA RTX 2080 / AMD RX 5700 XT (8 GB VRAM) |
| Storage | SSD — the sim is large and benefits enormously from fast storage |
| Internet | 20 Mbps+ (streaming terrain and weather) |

Lower-spec machines will run the sim but will need settings reduced. The in-game graphics settings have a significant impact on performance.

## Initial setup

### Graphics settings

On first launch, MSFS will offer an automated graphics preset. Accept it as a starting point, then adjust:

- **Render scaling** — the most impactful single setting; lower this first to recover frames
- **Terrain LOD / Object LOD** — reduce if you see stuttering over dense areas
- **Traffic** — AI aircraft and road vehicles are expensive; reduce if needed

### Control bindings

Before flying, bind your joystick, yoke, or controller:

1. Go to **Options → Controls**
2. Select your device from the list
3. Apply a preset profile (most major controllers have one)
4. Review the key bindings and adjust any that conflict with your hardware

At minimum, make sure you have: pitch, roll, yaw, throttle, and brakes bound.

### Assistance settings

If you are new to flight simulation, consider enabling some assistance options while you learn:

- **AI copilot assistance** — handles tasks you are not ready for yet
- **Landing assistance** — helps with glidepath awareness
- **Crash detection** — turn off if you want to focus on learning without interruptions

Reduce assistance over time as your skills improve.

## Your first flight

1. From the main menu, choose **World Map**
2. Search for a small airfield near a city you recognise — somewhere flat and familiar
3. Select a **Cessna 172** (or similar simple GA aircraft)
4. Set time of day to daytime, weather to **Clear skies**
5. Click **Fly**

On the ground, take a moment to look around the cockpit before touching anything. Identify the altimeter, airspeed indicator, vertical speed indicator, and heading indicator.

When ready:
- Apply full throttle
- Keep straight with rudder
- Lift off when the airspeed reaches around 60–65 knots
- Climb at 500–700 ft/min

Fly for 10–15 minutes, then return and land. Do not worry about making a perfect landing — getting the aircraft back near the runway is a success on your first attempt.
