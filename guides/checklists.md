# Checklists

Checklists are a core discipline in aviation. They ensure that critical actions are completed in the correct order, every time — regardless of distractions, familiarity with the aircraft, or how rushed you feel.

{: .warning }
All content on this page is for entertainment purposes in flight simulation only and is not intended for real-world use.

## Why use checklists?

In real aviation, checklists are mandatory. In simulation, they are optional — but using them is one of the fastest ways to improve the quality and realism of your flying.

Benefits:
- Prevents you forgetting steps like gear down on final or fuel selectors before take-off
- Gives each flight a structure that feels more like the real thing
- Makes it easier to hand control back and forth with a co-pilot
- Helps you learn an aircraft systematically

## Types of checklist

| Type | When used |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-flight | Before boarding — exterior walkaround |
| Before start | Aircraft powered up, ready to start engines |
| After start | Engines running, pre-taxi checks |
| Before taxi | Ready to move |
| Before take-off | Holding short of the runway |
| After take-off | Gear and flaps retracted, climb established |
| Cruise | Level at cruise altitude |
| Descent | Preparation for arrival |
| Approach | Before the final approach fix |
| Before landing | Gear down, flaps set, briefed |
| After landing | Runway vacated |
| Shutdown | Engines off, aircraft secured |

## Where to find checklists

### Built-in checklists (MSFS)

Most aircraft in MSFS include a checklist accessible from the toolbar. Press the checklist icon to open it. Quality varies — some are comprehensive, others are minimal.

### Third-party checklists

For detailed checklists matching the real aircraft procedures:

- **Manufacturer's operating handbooks** for GA aircraft are often available free online (e.g. Cessna 172 POH)
- **Simviation** and **Flightsim.to** host free checklist packs for popular aircraft
- Many payware aircraft include their own PDF checklists

### Building your own

For aircraft without a good checklist, writing your own is straightforward. The key items to include:

- Any item where forgetting could be catastrophic (fuel, gear, flaps, magnetos)
- Any item that must be completed before a phase of flight (engine start, departure, approach)
- Any item that is easy to forget because it is infrequent (pitot heat, beacon light, transponder mode)

## Using checklists effectively

**Challenge and response** is the standard method when flying with a co-pilot:
- Pilot monitoring reads the challenge: *"Flaps"*
- Pilot flying responds when actioned: *"Flaps set, 10°"*

When flying solo, read each item, complete the action, then verbally confirm it before moving on. Saying it out loud — even alone — reinforces the habit and prevents you just skimming down the list without actually doing the items.

**Flow patterns** are a complementary technique. A flow is a sweep of the cockpit in a logical visual order — left to right, top to bottom — that catches most items quickly. The checklist then confirms the flow was complete.

## The VFO airline and ACARS

The ACARS scoring system monitors certain aircraft states during your flight, including some that correspond directly to checklist items:

- Beacon lights must be on when engines are running
- Strobe lights must be on when airborne
- Landing lights off above 10,000 ft

Using a proper checklist keeps these in order naturally and protects your score. See the [Scoring guide](../operations/airline/scoring.md) for the full list of monitored items.
