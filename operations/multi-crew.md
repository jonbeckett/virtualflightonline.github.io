# Multi-crew flying

Multi-crew flying means two or more pilots operating together as a coordinated unit — whether in close formation, loose trail, or sharing duties on the same aircraft via an external tool. This page covers the practical side of flying with others in VFO.

## Formation flying

Formation flying is one of the most challenging and rewarding things you can do in simulation. It requires constant attention and clear communication.

### Formation types

| Formation | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Trail** | Aircraft follow directly behind the lead, offset slightly to one side. Easiest to maintain. |
| **Echelon** | Aircraft spread out diagonally to one side of the lead. Common in display flying. |
| **Vic** | Lead aircraft at front, two wingmen spread behind — a V shape. Classic military formation. |
| **Finger four** | Four aircraft arranged like the tips of an outstretched hand. Two elements of two. |

For VFO group flights, **trail** is the recommended formation for inexperienced pilots and large groups.

### Joining a formation

1. Approach from behind and below — never close from the lead's 3 or 9 o'clock
2. Match speed and heading before closing the gap
3. Stabilise your position before attempting to tighten up
4. Communicate your position to the lead before and during the join

### Holding position

Once in formation:

- **Reference a fixed point** on the lead aircraft — a wingtip, tailplane, or engine — and keep it in a fixed spot on your screen
- **Make small, smooth corrections** — large inputs cause oscillation
- **Match the lead's throttle changes** as soon as you see the aircraft pitch or accelerate
- **Never fixate** — keep a scan between your reference point, your airspeed, and the horizon

### Breaking away

Always break **away from the formation** — outwards, never through it. Call the break before you execute it: *"[Callsign] breaking right."*

### Communication discipline

Formation voice calls are brief and specific:

| Call | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *"Tighten up"* | Close the gaps, get closer to assigned position |
| *"Spread it out"* | Increase spacing |
| *"[Callsign] is visual"* | You have the lead in sight |
| *"[Callsign] is blind"* | You have lost sight of the lead — break away immediately |
| *"Smoke on"* | Enable wingtip effects / smoke (if available) |
| *"Joker"* | Low fuel state — pilot needs to leave the formation soon |
| *"Bingo"* | Minimum fuel to reach home — leaving formation now |

## Shared cockpit tools

Some third-party tools allow two pilots to fly the same aircraft from different computers. This is commonly called a "shared cockpit" or "co-pilot" session.

Tools that support this:

- **YourControls** (free) — designed for MSFS; allows full control sharing with a second pilot over the internet
- **vPilot / VATSIM** — focused on ATC comms but some pairing features

Shared cockpit is not part of standard VFO operations, but any two pilots can organise a session independently.

### Pilot flying vs. pilot monitoring

In a two-pilot cockpit, duties are split:

| Role | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| **Pilot flying (PF)** | Controls the aircraft — stick/yoke, thrust, rudder |
| **Pilot monitoring (PM)** | Radios, checklists, monitoring instruments, callouts |

A standard callout pattern: the PM calls "V1 … Rotate … V2 … Positive rate", and the PF responds "Gear up." Using these calls even in simulation builds good habits and makes shared cockpit sessions much smoother.
